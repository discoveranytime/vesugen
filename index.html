Vesugen
the vessel peptide

A 3-amino-acid peptide (Lys-Glu-Asp) from the Khavinson bioregulator family, aimed at blood vessels — the lining of every artery and vein you own. Cell studies show real effects on vessel cells; human evidence is two small Russian studies. Here is what it actually is, separated from the hype.

Quick read

Vesugen (KED) is a synthetic tripeptide bioregulator developed in St. Petersburg, described as vessel-wall-specific. In cell studies it normalized vessel-cell signaling, restored cell-to-cell connections, and switched on a cell-renewal gene — genuinely interesting biology. But human data is limited to two small, uncontrolled Russian studies, no Western lab has replicated the mechanism, and no regulator has approved it. Scroll for the full picture.

01The molecule

What Vesugen actually is

Three amino acids aimed at the tissue you can't live three minutes without. Vesugen (Lys-Glu-Asp) is the bioregulator program's vessel-wall peptide — pointed at the endothelium, the living lining of every blood vessel.

Each bead is one amino acid. Three beads aimed at the most critical lining in the body.

Vessel-specific (claimed)

The endothelium controls blood flow, clotting, and vessel repair. Vesugen is described as the peptide tuned to it.

Tiny molecule

390 g/mol — small even for a peptide. In theory, easier to absorb and distribute than its bigger cousins.

Supplement, not medicine

Sold as a supplement / research peptide. No regulator has evaluated it for safety or efficacy.

Plain English

Your vessels are lined with a living wallpaper — one cell thick, controlling everything that flows. Vesugen's text message reads "wallpaper: stay young." Three letters aimed at the plumbing of life itself.

02The vessel wall

The vessel wall

Vascular aging starts decades before symptoms — stiffening, less repair, worse regulation. Vesugen comes from the bioregulator program pointing at that slow decay.

From a vessel-wall extract to a defined tripeptide with named gene targets.
The endothelium bet

The vessel lining ages visibly by middle age — worse relaxation, worse repair. It's implicated in blood pressure, circulation, even cognitive decline.

Ventfort → Vesugen

First the natural vessel extract (Ventfort), then the defined tripeptide (Vesugen/KED) — the reproducible version of the idea.

One network's science

Plain English

Blood vessels are where aging becomes plumbing — and plumbing failures kill. Vesugen aims at the plumber's nightmare: the living lining itself. Good target, familiar problem: the science never left one lab network.

04Evidence check

What the science actually supports

Vesugen's file is the best of the bioregulator bunch: real cell biology with gene names, plus a whisper of human data. "Best in a thin field" is still thin.

Interesting cell biology

Named, measurable effects on vessel cells — normalized signals, restored junctions, a renewal gene switched on. The strongest cell-level story among the bioregulators here.

Two small human studies

Small, uncontrolled Russian studies in vascular patients. Better than zero — far from proof.

Single-network science

Nearly everything comes from one research network. Science trusts replication; Vesugen is still waiting for it.

Plain English

It's the one bioregulator here a skeptic should take most seriously — specific, measurable, biologically plausible. And still: one lab network, two tiny human studies, zero replication. "Most serious" and "proven" are different planets.

06Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

Vessel tone isn't a toy. What's known, what's theoretical, and what's blank.

Unregulated products

Observed fact: What's sold as Vesugen has no quality oversight. Supplement-grade peptides vary; independent verification is rare.

Unstudied at scale

Observed fact: Two small studies can't characterize side effects. The human safety file is effectively empty.

Vessel-tone meddling

Theoretical: Theoretical: tuning endothelin and vessel tone could interact with blood pressure and clotting — systems where small shifts matter.

The calorie-restriction echo

Theoretical: Vesugen is described as mimicking some calorie-restriction pathways (sirtuin-1) — intriguing, and another unmapped systemic effect.

Long-term vascular effects

Unknown: No chronic-use data. What months or years of vessel-signal tuning does is uncharted.

Interactions with heart meds

Unknown: Unknown: no interaction studies — a real concern for anyone on cardiovascular drugs, the very people likeliest to try it.

Plain English

Known: unstudied at scale, unregulated. Theoretical: blood-pressure and clotting systems are not toys. Unknown: everything long-term — plus drug interactions that matter most to the people likeliest to try it.
